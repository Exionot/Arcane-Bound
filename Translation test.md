The morning light in Fluxpoint City was always a clinical, filtered thing, struggling to pierce the heavy curtains of Aura's apartment. Inside the bedroom, the air was still thick with the lingering warmth of sleep. Aura stood by the bedside, already dressed and fueled by the restless energy of a scheduled Saturday. She looked down at the mound of blankets where Mint Evergreen was currently buried, a stray mint-green lock of hair the only sign of life.

"Come on, Mint," Aura urged, her voice carrying the practiced authority of a commander trying to rally a very reluctant troop. "If we don't leave now, the line for that cafe will be around the block. We won't get a seat, let alone those pastries you've been talking about all week."

A muffled groan drifted from beneath the duvet. "Mmmm... but it’s *so* early," Mint’s voice was thick with sleep, the words trailing off into a soft, feline huff. The blankets shifted slightly as she burrowed deeper into the mattress, clearly declaring her allegiance to the pillows.

Aura sighed, a small, fond smile tugging at the corner of her mouth despite her frustration. She knew this battle; Mint’s sleepiness was a primal force, an undefeated opponent. "Fine," Aura said, turning on her heel with a theatrical flair. "If you plan to just lay there and rot all day, I’ll just go to the cafe myse—"

She didn't finish the sentence. 

A hand shot out from the tangle of sheets, fingers locking around Aura’s wrist with surprising strength. Before the former soldier could find her footing, Mint yanked. Aura gasped as the world tilted; she stumbled, losing her balance and falling directly onto the bed—and more specifically, directly onto the soft, warm weight of a very awake Mint Evergreen.

Aura froze. She was pinned by the sudden gravity of the moment, her hands braced against the mattress on either side of Mint’s head. Below her, Mint opened her eyes. They were bright, clear, and devoid of the "sleepiness" she had just been claiming as a shield. Mint stared up at her, watching the way Aura’s breath hitched and the way a deep, traitorous flush began to creep up Aura’s neck.

"What's wrong?" Mint asked. Her voice was no longer muffled; it was low, teasing, and vibrated right through Aura’s chest.

For a long moment, the only sound was the hum of the apartment's climate control. Finally, they both sat up, the bed creaking under the shift in weight. Aura looked away, shaking her head as if trying to physically dislodge the thoughts racing through her mind. 

"Sorry," Aura managed, her voice uncharacteristically small. "My mind was being... a little greedy, I think. It’s just... whenever I look at y—"

She never got to finish that thought either. 

Mint leaned in, closing the distance before Aura could even blink. The kiss was sudden, soft, and tasted faintly of the lingering dreams of the morning. It cut Aura’s confession short, replacing her logic with a sudden, sharp intake of breath. When Mint finally pulled back, just an inch, she wore a mischievous, lopsided grin.

"I feel a little greedy, too," Mint whispered, her eyes dancing with a light that had nothing to do with the sun. Then, as quickly as the moment had sparked, she added with a playful wink, "...and also sleepy!"

With the grace of a practiced acrobat, Mint flopped backward and tucked herself back into the duvet, disappearing into a cocoon of fabric before Aura could even process the contact.

"!?... Hey!" Aura sputtered, her heart still hammering against her ribs like a trapped bird. "Don't you dare fall back asleep after that! Mint!"

The only response was a soft, contented hum from beneath the blankets. The commander had been defeated again, not by a soldier, but by a girl who knew exactly how to use a kiss to win a morning nap.
