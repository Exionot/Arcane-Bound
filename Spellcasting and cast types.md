Everybody has their own spellcasting style. Just like how two artist can draw the same exact person yet their outputs look different.

Since the entire magic system relies on belief, the way a spell looks when casted is primarily based on how the caster imagines it.

Besides spells looking different depending on the caster, there are also different ways to cast spells:
### Hand Gestures
- Requires the caster to execute hand gestures
- Mainly used in Astraxia (Magic without a staff)
- Allows precision for spells that require gestures similar to writing
- Common for spells that require physical touch like healing magic

### Staff Gestures
- Requires the caster to do gestures using their staff to cast
- Mainly used in Arcana (Magic with a staff)
##### Casting from the staff head
- Spells where the final gesture is to point the staff outwards toward a target
- Common for ranged spells like fireball (though not exclusive)
##### Casting from the staff base
- Spells where the final gesture is to hit the ground using the base of the staff
- Common for AOE spells
- Spell output may be different depending on the staff's structure:
	- Staffs with a flat base targets the actual ground (i.e. releasing a shockwave outwards that shakes the ground)
	- Staffs with a pointed base will never hit the ground when casting an AOE spell, only hover inches from the ground. This targets the surrounding area of the caster.

### Combo Gestures
- Requires the caster to do a hand and staff gesture in quick succession
- Most commonly used in lingering AOE spells
- The first gesture is usually a hovering spell for the staff (hand gesture) then the AOE spell (staff gesture). The caster then holds the AOE spell for as long as they want the spell to remain active. This will leave the caster stationary and vulnerable.
- Usually done by support mages

### Telepathic Gestures
- A highly advanced execution of Astraxia
- Requires the caster to only think of the spell they want to cast
- It also allows the caster to use their staff from far away without holding it
